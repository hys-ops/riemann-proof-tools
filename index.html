Mathematical research notes

Proof tools from a Riemann-hypothesis manuscript

A collection of local proof tools and their applications: recover the information a later step needs before estimating or rebuilding more than necessary.

Derived from work on a pinned public manuscript. This collection does not prove the Riemann hypothesis, establish the manuscript’s global correctness, or assert priority for the underlying algebraic identities.

Where the tools led

  1. Manuscript: a one-sided derivative inverts the piecewise-linear smoothing of character sums.
  2. Direct reuse: the same operator recovers the signed tail of two finite multisets; the written proof of Chen’s consecutive-power bound then uses sign changes, variation and Chebyshev polynomials.
  3. Another direct application: for lower semilinear copulas, the same operator measures the defect in Chatterjee’s correlation under uniform convergence.
  4. Separate downstream tool: the 11-term Prouhet–Tarry–Escott search motivated a two-point reflection contact criterion for rational decomposition. Neither the real endpoint theorem nor this criterion settles existence of an ideal 11-term integer solution.
  5. Inspiration only: Kendall uniqueness uses Williamson’s representation and a weighted quantile argument, not this differential identity.

How to read verification labels

Every PDF proves the displayed standalone mechanism. A manuscript application may depend on supplied analytic, arithmetic or host assumptions; these are not promoted to verified unconditional results. A focused Lean check or archived run is not a full verification of the paper. The later Young-diagram reconstruction is an independent discrete identity, not another application of the smoothing differential operator.
