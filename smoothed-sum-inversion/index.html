Proof tool 1 of 6

Invert a piecewise-linear smoothing

Exact identity · Direct transfer to equal power sums

Read the standalone proof (PDF)

Input. Finite signed atoms at heights s, a linear term or endpoint anchor, and the one-sided derivative convention. Reconstruction from a general target requires local absolute continuity, not just continuity.

Output. A weighted step function and, from its jumps, signed atoms. With an anchor, reconstruct the continuous profile. The inverse determines the profile only modulo a term proportional to t when there is no anchor.

The inversion

For G(t)=ct+Σₛwₛ(t/s−1)₊, with all s positive, the right derivative includes the terms at s=t. Hence tG′₊(t)−G(t)=P(t)=Σ_{s≤t} wₛ: each active term contributes wₛ and the linear term cancels. The left derivative gives Σ_{s<t} wₛ. Finite knots give a continuous, locally absolutely continuous G.

Away from knots, (G/t)′=P/t². On a positive interval, integrating yields G(t)=(t/B)G(B)−t∫ₜᴮ P(u)/u² du. The formula extends across knots by continuity. Conversely, the integral reconstruction has the stated derivative away from knots and the same endpoint; any locally absolutely continuous difference with (G/t)′=0 a.e. equals ct. To design a smooth profile H, first target the raw sum P*=tH′−H; realizing that target by arithmetic data remains a separate theorem.

What this changes in the manuscript

For each height n use wₙ=Λ(n)χ(n), where Λ is the von Mangoldt function, and put the log-derivative term in c. In the selected fixed-modulus M=1 construction, one first prescribes H and targets P*=tH′−H via prime selection. This avoids differentiating an error bound. The known window construction also needs the independent prime-selection inputs, fixed K>1, 0<ε<1/52 and sufficiently large Y. PrimePair.SignedSlope.affine_cell is a local Lean check for an affine cell, not a proof of prime-pair existence.

Exact transfer: Chen’s largest-variable problem

Let A and B be multisets of N≥2 real numbers, normalized to minimum 0 and maximum 1, with max A=1>max B=b. Their first N−1 power sums agree. For μ=Σₐδₐ−Σᵦδᵦ, use H(t)=∫min(1,t/s)dμ(s) for t>0, with kernel value 1 at s=0. Now tH′₊−H=−μ([0,t])=μ((t,1])=:D(t); the last equality needs μ([0,1])=0. The negative jumps of D extended by zero recover the signed atoms, including at 0 and 1.

Equal powers mean ∫₀¹ D(t)tʲ dt=0 for j=0,…,N−2, by integrating each power from 0 up to its atom. A nonzero step function orthogonal to these weights has at least N−1 sign changes: if it had ℓ≤N−2, place a root of a polynomial at each sign transition (in each intervening zero stretch if present); orient the degree-ℓ product to agree with all nonzero signs. Its product with the function would integrate to a strictly positive number.

Extending D by zero, its variation is at most 2N, one unit per signed atom before cancellation. Choose one value from each of N alternating nonzero blocks. Travel from zero through these values and back costs twice the sum of their absolute values, at least 2N. Thus |D|≤1, with exactly N nonzero sign blocks; any extra sign block or separated same-sign excursion would exceed the budget. Near 1 and in fact on (b,1), D=1 by this bound.

For a comparator take A* and B* as the +1 and −1 fibers of T_N(2t−1). The endpoint 1 is in A*, the largest B* entry is c=(1+cos(π/N))/2, and each successive interior extremum (1+cos(jπ/N))/2 has multiplicity two with alternating signs. The endpoint 0 is simple. The two monic root polynomials differ only by a constant; Newton’s identities give equal first N−1 powers. Thus the resulting D* takes alternating ±1 on exactly N consecutive intervals and equals +1 above c.

If b≤c, then D−D* is zero on the last comparator interval. On each remaining comparator interval it has a fixed weak sign since |D|≤1. It is orthogonal to degrees through N−2, but can change sign at most N−2 times, so the same root-product argument forces D=D* almost everywhere. Their jumps recover μ; the saturated variation excludes common atoms, giving uniqueness up to ordering. Hence b≥c=cos²(π/(2N)), with equality precisely for the comparator multisets. For N=3: A=(1,1/4,1/4), B=(3/4,3/4,0).

Beyond consecutive powers

For N−1 distinct positive real exponents kᵢ, orthogonality is instead against the integrable weights t^(kᵢ−1). A nonzero combination of m of these weights has at most m−1 interior zeros counted with multiplicity: divide by the smallest power, differentiate, and apply Rolle’s theorem inductively. This replaces the polynomial in the sign-change proof above. Choose an auxiliary nonnegative exponent p distinct from the weight exponents, and take a best L¹ approximation v to t^p in the weight span. A minimizer exists by finite-dimensional norm equivalence and coercivity. The residual R=t^p−v has at most N−1 zeros, and variation in any span direction w yields ∫sign(R)w=0 by dominated convergence. Thus the sign profile has exactly N−1 switches. Orient it to be positive near 1.

The negatives of its jumps after zero extension give two N-atom multisets with the required equal powers. Its last switch is the largest entry of B. The same comparison with D as above forces every feasible b at or below that last switch to equal it, and forces their signed atoms to match. The resulting switch locations, not the cosine expression, describe the sharp general bound. This is the direct operator route. In the consecutive case one can also give an independent proof by starting with the two root polynomials that differ by a constant and studying their Chebyshev extremum; that route uses no differential operator.

Verification boundary

The Chen endpoint result above is a written proof; the archived Lean run covered only the finite integral-path variation lemma, ChenAudit.saturated_alternations. It did not certify the inversion, moment oscillation, or cosine endpoint as a complete theorem. Later 11-term integer investigations were a separate downstream problem; no new ideal 11-term integer solution is claimed here. Kendall uniqueness was inspired by recovery from a smoothed observable, but uses Williamson’s representation and weighted quantile comparisons, not this differential identity.
