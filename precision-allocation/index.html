Exact change of coordinates · Written arithmetic application

Read the standalone proof (PDF)

Input. A fixed linear readout of several quantities and the independent constraints that still apply to each quantity.

Output. The subspace requiring fine control, the directions invisible to that readout, and separate budgets for any individual constraints.

Proof

For A=g_q−g_d−g_qd put U=(g_d+g_qd)/2 and V=(g_d−g_qd)/2. Then A=g_q−2U exactly; g_d=U+V and g_qd=U−V. If g_q is within ε_q of its target and U within ε_U of its target, the readout is within ε_q+2ε_U of its target. Changing V leaves that bound unchanged. But if both individual rows must satisfy |g|≤B, one sufficient condition remains |U|+|V|≤B.

A concrete witness separates the constraints: with targets g_d=g_qd=U₀, take U=U₀ and |V|>ε. Both rows miss their old ε target even though the signed readout is exact; with |U₀|+|V|≤B the coarse row constraints still hold. This finite witness illustrates why weakening the exact target is not the same as dropping a coarse condition.

Application in the November 20 release

In the manuscript’s signed combination, the q row and the mean of the two second-prime rows need the narrow budgets. The local proof switches to two sign classes to assign the mean and difference. With the stated arithmetic choice and exceptional-set estimates, it constructs one pair meeting the new readout and coarse constraints even when old separate fine constraints fail. The latter is a separate arithmetic argument, not a consequence of the coordinate change.

Verification boundary

The archived focused Lean 4.34.1 check lists nine finite statements, including signed_readout, signed_anchor_error, weighted_good_outside_bad and row_gap. It does not establish existence of the prime pair unconditionally. No general replacement of PairGood is asserted.

The staged proof is the one-height algebra. Height-dependent applications must also control transition errors and separately prove realization across the window.
