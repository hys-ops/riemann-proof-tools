Research path · Equal power sums

The 11-term equal-power-sum investigation

An unsuccessful integer construction produced a useful test for functional decomposition.

Previous step: Chen’s signed cumulative function and its jumps

What carried over from Chen

Chen’s argument recovered a signed cumulative function and read its jumps to recover the entries and multiplicities. The 11-term search reused the cumulative-and-jump step: shift the cumulative function by an integer h and subtract the shifted copy. It did not reapply the differential operator.

Start with two 10-term integer lists A and B with equal power sums through degree 9. The shifted difference gives two 20-term lists: A together with B+h, and B together with A+h. A difference of a degree-k polynomial and its translate has degree at most k−1, so the new lists agree through degree 10. Cancelling nine common entries would leave 11 on each side. The tested constructions never reached nine. This neither constructs an ideal 11-term integer solution nor proves none exists.

What came out of the search

In a separate symmetric construction, fixing five distinct nonzero absolute values left six unknowns in one fiber of a degree-six rational map f. The graphs of f(x) and f(−x) meet only at 0 and infinity, with contact multiplicities 1 and 11. The reflection-contact test says that for a map of degree d, contact at only two points, of orders 1 and 2d−1 rule out a composition with both factors of degree at least two. Here this excluded the 2-by-3 and 3-by-2 decompositions. It did not exclude the fiber itself.

The same test was later used on a quartic cover and on a balanced auxiliary polynomial in the Tutte investigation. Neither use is a prerequisite for the final all-rank proof.

Research notes. No new ideal 11-term integer solution or priority claim is made.
