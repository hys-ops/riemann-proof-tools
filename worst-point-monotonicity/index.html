Proof tool 5 of 6

Find the worst point after normalization

Exact derivative test · Written selected-pair result

Read the standalone proof (PDF)

Input. A continuous, piecewise differentiable nonvanishing g on [Y,B], and the actual exponent α in the bound. For a piecewise-linear profile, use its exact slopes away from knots.

Output. An endpoint test on the normalized quantity when its derivative has one sign. A bound on g alone does not imply this sign.

Proof

On a differentiable piece, t times the derivative of log(|g(t)|/t^α) equals Re(tg′(t)/g(t))−α. If this is strictly negative on every piece, the normalized ratio decreases there. Continuity across the finitely many knots orders the pieces, so its maximum is at Y. If the sign is positive, the maximum is at B. For finite smoothing, use t g′=g+P on each interior piece rather than differentiating an asymptotic error.

Application in the November 20 release

On the selected signed prime pair in the written M=1 analysis, the exact raw-sum and smoothed-value estimates yield Re(tg′/g)=1/2+o(1), uniformly on the applicable window. With α=1/2+τ for fixed τ>0 and sufficiently large Y, the maximum of |g|/t^α is at Y. For α=1/2 itself the displayed precision decides no sign, and changed target pairs can behave differently. For the corresponding selected signed signal the derivative can be positive while its individual normalized upper-bound ratios decrease.

Verification boundary

The calculus argument is explicit. The uniform arithmetic estimates and the selected-pair reduction are written, not an end-to-end Lean result. The reduction covers the scalar bounds it analyzes, not separate zero-box conditions or an arbitrary prime pair.
